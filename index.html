Case Notes

Why These Seven Files

True Alibi already speaks in red velvet, candlelight, wax seals and elegant French phrasing. That's how you already do it, long before any ad runs. This deck borrows that same visual world and gives it seven different reasons to make a stranger stop scrolling.

Each file pairs a psychological driver with a video concept and a hook sharp enough to open a campaign. Test them independently on Meta, then build the strongest performers into a full Halloween push.

Evidence

The Case Files

Seven territories. Seven reasons someone would want in.

01Dossier
Psychological Driver
Paranoia, friendship dynamics

Your Friends Are Lying

Friends gather for an elegant Halloween dinner. Each gets a character, a secret, a motive. A normal night turns into suspicion: friends hide information, form alliances, accuse each other, until the murderer turns out to be sitting right at the table.

Why It's Strong

This isn't about solving a murder. It's about friends who suddenly can't trust each other. Viewers instantly picture their own circle: who'd lie best, who'd fool everyone. A fast, personal hook, strong for cold acquisition.

Hook

One of your friends is the murderer. Everyone has something to hide.

02Dossier
Psychological Driver
Transformation, anti‑boring

Cancel the Normal Halloween Party

Opens on a familiar Halloween party: costumes, drinks, music, photos. Fun, but predictable. Then it turns: guests get characters and secrets, a murder happens, accusations fly, and the ordinary party becomes a story everyone is living inside.

Why It's Strong

True Alibi isn't competing with other murder mystery games here. It's competing with the normal Halloween party, which opens the audience to anyone tired of doing the same thing again this year.

Hook

Cancel your normal Halloween party. Costumes, drinks, small talk again?

03Dossier
Psychological Driver
Identity, escapism, roleplay

Don't Dress Up. Become Someone Else.

People prepare for Halloween, but instead of a costume they get a new character: a mysterious heiress, someone with a dangerous past, a blackmailer, a prime suspect. By the dinner table, everyone has a new identity and a reason to lie.

Why It's Strong

Halloween already gives people permission to become someone else. True Alibi deepens that fantasy, turning a party game into a premium roleplay experience with higher perceived value.

Hook

A costume changes how you look. True Alibi changes who you are.

04Dossier
Psychological Driver
Challenge, competition, ego

How Well Do You Know Your Friends?

One friend is lying. Another is hiding something. Someone's story doesn't match the evidence. The viewer becomes the investigator, testing whether they could actually spot who's lying.

Why It's Strong

Turns entertainment into a social challenge. People believe they can read their own friends, and that belief pulls them in before they even click.

Hook

How well do you really know your friends? Would you know if one was lying?

05Dossier
Psychological Driver
FOMO, social currency

The Party They'll Talk About Tomorrow

Opens the morning after Halloween: the group chat still going over who lied best and who nobody suspected. Then a flashback to the night itself, the accusations, the clues, the reveal.

Why It's Strong

Sells the story that outlives the party, not just the party itself. Combines FOMO and social proof, and fits a native, social-first cut better than a polished cinematic one.

Hook

The best parties don't end when everyone goes home.

06Dossier
Psychological Driver
Cinematic fantasy, aspiration

Don't Watch the Thriller. Enter It.

Feels at first like a premium thriller: a mansion, a masked dinner, a murder, suspicious guests. Then the reveal: the people watching are suddenly part of it, and real friends become the characters.

Why It's Strong

The most aspirational file, and the strongest use of AI production in service of a real idea, not just visuals. Instead of watching the story, you step inside it. Best candidate for the hero creative.

Hook

Don't watch the thriller. Enter it.

07Dossier
Psychological Driver
Simplicity, removing friction

Even the Host Doesn't Know

Someone plans a Halloween dinner and invites friends over. It looks like they'll have to run the whole game. Instead, the host gets their own character too, becoming just as much a suspect as everyone else.

Why It's Strong

Removes a real objection: people who love the idea but assume it's complicated to organize. Useful for retargeting, further down the funnel.

Hook

She planned the party. Not the murder.

How the Files Connect

Not seven versions of one Halloween ad. A testing system built to find seven different reasons to want True Alibi.

01 · Your Friends Are LyingTests distrust between friends, the strongest social mechanism.
02 · Cancel the Normal PartyTests a broader need: people who've never looked for a murder mystery.
03 · Become Someone ElseTests identity, escapism and roleplay.
04 · Know Your Friends?Tests ego: the urge to prove you can read people.
05 · Talk About TomorrowTests FOMO and shared memory.
06 · Enter the ThrillerTests cinematic aspiration. Strongest hero creative candidate.
07 · Even the Host Doesn't KnowRemoves the objection of perceived complexity, lower funnel.
